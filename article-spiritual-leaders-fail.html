When Spiritual Leaders Fail You: How Do You Keep Going?

How do you keep loving Christ and His church when spiritual leaders have hurt you?

September 30, 2026

When Spiritual Leaders Fail You: How Do You Keep Going?

There is a particular kind of hurt that comes from watching a pastor fall.

And then there is another kind of hurt that comes from watching a pastor handle that fall wrongly.

I have now experienced both.

I have watched a pastor whose preaching I loved fall into grievous sexual sin.

And I have watched another pastor respond to serious sin with gossip, falsehood, accusations, and what appeared to be an effort to protect another pastor from the consequences of his actions.

The first experience shook my faith.

The second tested my trust.

And after going through both, I found myself asking a question I don’t think I am the only Christian to have asked:

How do you keep going?

How do you keep loving the church when church leaders have hurt you?

How do you listen to a sermon without wondering what is happening behind the scenes?

How do you trust a pastor without becoming naive?

How do you honour spiritual authority without putting people on pedestals?

How do you forgive without pretending nothing happened?

How do you refuse bitterness without calling evil good?

And perhaps most importantly:

How do you walk away from all of this without walking away from Christ?

First, You Remember Who Actually Saved You

This sounds obvious.

But when someone you’ve respected spiritually falls, it can become surprisingly easy to confuse the messenger with the message.

I loved the preaching.

I loved the teaching.

I learned things from these men.

God used their ministries in my life.

But neither of them saved me.

Neither of them died for me.

Neither of them is my mediator.

Neither of them is my Shepherd.

Christ is.

That doesn’t make what happened insignificant.

But it puts it back into its proper place.

A pastor can fail without the gospel failing.

A pastor can lie without Scripture becoming a lie.

A pastor can fall into sexual sin without Christ becoming less holy.

A pastor can mishandle church discipline without God’s Word becoming less true.

The foundation underneath us hasn’t moved.

Christ hasn’t fallen.

Second, You Stop Expecting Pastors to Be What Only Christ Can Be

I think sometimes we unknowingly ask too much of pastors.

We expect them to be examples—and Scripture does call them to be examples.

We expect them to be faithful—and they should be.

We expect them to be trustworthy—and they should be.

But somewhere along the way, we can begin expecting them to be incapable of failure.

They aren’t.

They’re men.

They’re sinners saved by grace.

They need the same gospel they preach.

They need accountability.

They need correction.

They need brothers who will tell them when they’re wrong.

They need to repent.

And sometimes they need to be removed from positions of leadership.

That doesn’t make the biblical office of pastor worthless.

It reminds us that the office is sacred precisely because the men occupying it are not.

Third, Don’t Let Someone Else’s Sin Become Your Excuse to Sin

This one is hard.

Because when you’ve been hurt by the church, bitterness can feel justified.

You start thinking:

“After everything I’ve seen, why should I care anymore?”

“If pastors can behave like this, what’s the point?”

“Why should I bother with church?”

“Everyone is a hypocrite anyway.”

And little by little, the sin committed against you begins producing sin in you.

Their gossip produces bitterness.

Their lies produce suspicion.

Their hypocrisy produces cynicism.

Their lack of repentance produces your unwillingness to forgive.

And suddenly, their sin has begun determining your spiritual life.

We cannot allow that.

Their sin is theirs.

What we do with it before God is ours.

Fourth, Learn the Difference Between Discernment and Cynicism

I think this is one of the hardest balances.

After you’ve been lied about, you become more alert to lies.

After you’ve seen sin covered up, you become more suspicious of what happens behind closed doors.

After you’ve watched people defend the indefensible, you become slower to trust.

Some of that may actually be wisdom.

The Bible doesn’t call us to be gullible.

Jesus told His disciples to be wise as serpents and innocent as doves.

The Bereans examined what they were taught.

Paul told believers to test everything.

Discernment is not a lack of love.

But there is a point where discernment can become cynicism.

You begin assuming everyone is guilty before there is evidence.

You stop believing anything anyone says.

You interpret every mistake as evidence of corruption.

You begin looking for the next scandal.

You stop seeing the church as Christ’s bride and start seeing only everything wrong with it.

That isn’t freedom.

That’s another kind of bondage.

So I think the prayer has to be:

“Lord, give me discernment without making me cynical.”

Help me recognize sin without becoming obsessed with it.

Help me recognize falsehood without becoming suspicious of everyone.

Help me forgive without becoming foolish.

Help me trust without becoming naive.

Fifth, Don’t Abandon the Local Church Because People Have Failed You

This may be the hardest one.

Because sometimes the instinct is:

“I’m done.”

And I understand that instinct.

Sometimes you need to step away from an unhealthy church.

Sometimes boundaries are necessary.

Sometimes leaving is the right thing to do.

But leaving an unhealthy leadership situation is not the same thing as leaving Christ’s church altogether.

Don’t let one pastor’s failure convince you that there are no faithful pastors.

Don’t let one church’s dysfunction convince you that every church is corrupt.

Don’t let one experience become your theology of the entire body of Christ.

There are pastors quietly opening their Bibles every Sunday and faithfully preaching Christ.

There are elders who take accountability seriously.

There are churches where sin is confronted rather than hidden.

There are believers who will love you without controlling you.

There are churches where you can worship Christ without becoming part of someone’s personality cult.

They exist.

And you need Christian fellowship.

You need God’s people.

You need the ordinary means of grace.

You need the Word.

You need prayer.

You need fellowship.

You need communion.

You need the encouragement of other believers.

Isolation can feel safe after you’ve been hurt.

But healing doesn’t always happen in isolation.

Sixth, Let Yourself Grieve

I think Christians sometimes feel guilty for being hurt by church situations.

We tell ourselves:

“I should just forgive.”

“I should move on.”

“I should trust God.”

And all of those things can be true while you are still grieving.

You lost something.

Maybe you lost trust.

Maybe you lost friendships.

Maybe you lost a church family.

Maybe you lost a pastor you once admired.

Maybe you lost the picture you had in your mind of what ministry was supposed to look like.

Those things are worth grieving.

Jesus Himself wept.

Grief isn’t unbelief.

You don’t have to rush yourself through it.

Bring it to God.

Tell Him you’re angry.

Tell Him you’re hurt.

Tell Him you don’t understand.

Tell Him that you’re afraid.

The Psalms are full of prayers from people who were hurting and confused.

God isn’t threatened by our honest prayers.

Seventh, Don’t Make One Person’s Fall the Measure of Christ’s Faithfulness

This is perhaps the most important thing I have learned.

A pastor’s faithfulness can change.

Christ’s doesn’t.

A church can disappoint you.

Christ doesn’t.

A leader can lie.

Christ cannot.

A shepherd can abandon his calling.

The Good Shepherd lays down His life for His sheep.

People can manipulate.

Christ doesn’t.

People can gossip.

Christ speaks truth.

People can abandon you.

Christ promises never to leave His people.

So when everything around you feels unstable, go back to the things that haven’t changed.

Open your Bible.

Read the Gospels.

Look at Christ.

Not the Christian celebrity.

Not the pastor.

Not the denomination.

Not the controversy.

Christ.

Look at His character.

Look at His holiness.

Look at His compassion.

Look at His patience.

Look at His cross.

Look at His resurrection.

Look at His promises.

The more clearly you see Christ, the less your faith depends on the performance of His servants.

Eighth, Forgive—but Don’t Confuse Forgiveness With Trust

This is another lesson I am still learning.

Forgiveness doesn’t mean pretending the person didn’t hurt you.

It doesn’t mean saying the behaviour was acceptable.

It doesn’t mean you have to restore the same level of trust.

And it certainly doesn’t mean putting someone back into a position where they can hurt people again.

Forgiveness releases vengeance to God.

Trust is rebuilt through demonstrated faithfulness.

Those are different things.

You can forgive someone and still maintain a boundary.

You can forgive someone and still believe accountability is necessary.

You can forgive someone and still say:

“You cannot shepherd me anymore.”

That isn’t hatred.

Sometimes it is wisdom.

Ninth, Don’t Let the Desire for Justice Consume You

When you’ve been falsely accused, there is a powerful desire to make everyone know the truth.

You want every person who heard the rumour to hear the correction.

You want everyone to know what really happened.

You want the people who lied about you to be exposed.

You want justice.

I understand that.

But there is a point where defending yourself becomes another form of bondage.

Sometimes you have to say:

God knows.

God knows what was said.

God knows what happened.

God knows what wasn’t said.

God knows who lied.

God knows who was faithful.

God knows who repented.

God knows who refused to repent.

God knows the whole story.

And sometimes that has to be enough.

Romans 12:19 tells us:

“Beloved, never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God.” — Romans 12:19

That doesn’t mean we never speak.

It doesn’t mean we never seek justice.

It doesn’t mean we allow abuse or wrongdoing to continue.

It means that vengeance isn’t our job.

God doesn’t need our anger to accomplish His purposes.

And Finally, Keep Walking

Even if your faith feels bruised.

Even if you don’t understand everything.

Even if you have questions.

Even if church doesn’t feel the same anymore.

Keep walking.

Open the Bible.

Pray.

Worship.

Gather with believers.

Take communion.

Serve.

Love people.

Repent when you sin.

Ask forgiveness when you hurt someone.

Refuse gossip.

Tell the truth.

Be faithful in the small things.

And keep your eyes on Christ.

Because perhaps the greatest victory over what happened isn’t becoming louder, angrier, or more cynical.

Perhaps it is becoming more faithful.

They can fail.

I don’t have to.

They can gossip.

I can choose truth.

They can protect sin.

I can choose righteousness.

They can become bitter.

I don’t have to.

They can walk away from Christ.

I don’t have to follow them.

I Don’t Know What the Next Few Months Will Look Like

Honestly, these past months have been terrifying.

I’ve had to process things I never expected to have to process.

I’ve had to rethink people I trusted.

I’ve had to wrestle with anger.

I’ve had to wrestle with grief.

I’ve had to ask questions I didn’t want to ask.

But I don’t want this experience to make me love Christ less.

I don’t want someone else’s failure to steal my joy in the gospel.

I don’t want someone’s lies to turn me into a liar.

I don’t want someone’s bitterness to make me bitter.

I don’t want someone else’s unfaithfulness to make me unfaithful.

And I don’t want what happened in a church to make me walk away from the One who gave Himself for the church.

So perhaps the prayer isn’t:

“Lord, give me a church where no one will ever hurt me.”

That church doesn’t exist.

Perhaps the prayer is:

“Lord, when people fail me, keep me faithful to You.”

Keep my heart soft.

Keep my eyes open.

Keep my feet steady.

Keep me from bitterness.

Keep me from naivety.

Keep me from cynicism.

Keep me from pride.

Keep me near Your Word.

And when I am tempted to give up, remind me:

I didn’t follow Christ because a pastor was faithful.

I followed Christ because Christ is faithful.

And He still is.

He always will be.
