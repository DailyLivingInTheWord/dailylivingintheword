When a Pastor Falls

What happens to our faith when someone we respected, trusted, and learned from falls into serious sin?

September 30, 2026

I never knew Steven Lawson personally. But what happened had a great impact on my life, both before and after his sin became public.

Before the news broke, I had loved his messages. I was actually going through his Romans series when the news of his adultery became public.

I was crushed in my spirit.

It is difficult to explain what happens inside you when someone you respected spiritually falls into serious sin. There can be grief, confusion, disappointment, anger, and even fear.

And for a moment, a frightening question can enter your mind:

“If a man of God can fall, what’s stopping a simple woman like me?”

But as I processed what had happened, I began to realize something important.

My faith had never been meant to rest on a man.

Not on a pastor.

Not on a preacher.

Not on an author.

Not even on someone whose teaching had helped me deeply.

We Must Be Careful Who We Build Our Faith On

God gives the church pastors, teachers, and faithful ministers of the Word. We should be thankful for them. We should listen to sound teaching, learn from godly examples, and honor those who faithfully labor for Christ.

But none of these people are Christ.

Every pastor is still a sinner saved by grace. Every teacher is still dependent upon the mercy of God. Every Christian leader is capable of falling.

This does not excuse sin. It does not minimize the damage caused by serious sin. And it does not mean that every fallen leader should immediately return to ministry.

But it does remind us where our ultimate confidence belongs.

The Word of God Does Not Fall

People fall.

Leaders fall.

Churches can disappoint us.

People we admired can fail us.

But God's Word remains true.

Scripture does not become less trustworthy because someone who preached it failed to live according to it.

The truth of Romans does not change because the man preaching Romans sinned.

The gospel does not become less glorious because a preacher dishonored the gospel he proclaimed.

Christ is not diminished by the failures of His servants.

Looking Beyond the Messenger

There is a danger in Christian culture of becoming deeply attached to particular teachers.

We can begin by appreciating a man's ministry and slowly move toward building our spiritual lives around that person.

We quote them constantly.

We listen to everything they preach.

We recommend all their books.

And without realizing it, we can begin to associate their voice with truth itself.

That is dangerous.

The Bereans were commended because they examined the Scriptures to see whether what they were being taught was true.

Our responsibility is not simply to ask, “Did my favorite preacher say it?”

We must ask, “Does Scripture say it?”

When Someone You Respected Falls

I have learned that this is not merely a theoretical issue.

There are moments when people close to us, or people we have looked up to spiritually, can be exposed as living in serious sin.

Those moments hurt.

They can make you question what you thought you knew.

They can make you wonder whether you can trust anyone.

But we must resist the temptation to throw away everything simply because a person failed.

Instead, we go back to Scripture.

We go back to Christ.

We go back to the gospel.

Forgiveness Does Not Mean Pretending Nothing Happened

Another important lesson is that forgiveness and restoration are not identical.

Christians are called to forgive.

But forgiveness does not mean ignoring sin, denying harm, removing necessary boundaries, or automatically restoring someone to a position of spiritual leadership.

Restoration to fellowship and restoration to a particular ministry position are separate questions that require wisdom, repentance, accountability, and careful consideration.

Grace never requires us to call sin something other than sin.

Christ Remains

Perhaps the greatest lesson I have learned through all of this is painfully simple:

People change.

People fail.

People disappoint us.

But Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.

“Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever.”

Hebrews 13:8

When someone we trusted falls, we may grieve. We may need time to process what happened. We may need to acknowledge the seriousness of the sin and the damage it caused.

But we do not need to lose Christ.

Because Christ was never the person who failed us.

He was never the one who changed.

He was never the one whose character was uncertain.

Our hope was never supposed to be in a man.

Our hope is in Christ.

Be Careful Who You Build Your Faith On

Learn from faithful teachers. Listen to sound preaching. Read good books. Thank God for pastors who faithfully proclaim His Word.

But do not build your faith on them.

Build your faith on Christ and His Word.

Because when everything else shakes, He remains.

And when a servant falls, the Master has not fallen.

When a messenger fails, the message of Scripture has not failed.

When people disappoint us, Christ remains faithful.

So let us keep our eyes fixed on Him.

To Christ.
